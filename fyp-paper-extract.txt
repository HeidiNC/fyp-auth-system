Abstract - Counterfeit products continue to pose a significant challenge in modern supply chains, leading to 
financial losses and potential safety risks. Conventional authentication methods, such as QR codes and 
centralized databases, are vulnerable to duplication, data manipulation, and the absence of effective 
revocation mechanisms, which reduces consumer trust. This paper proposes an enhanced product 
authentication system that applies blockchain -based principles with integrated clone detection and product 
revocation features. Each product is assigned a unique code linked to a tamper-evident record to support data 
integrity and traceability. Scan activities are monitored to identify abnormal patterns that may indicate 
duplicated codes. A revocation mechanism enables invalidation of compromised or counterfeit products , 
ensuring accurate verification results. The system is implemented as a web -based prototype and evaluated 
based on functionality, verification accuracy, and system response. The results show that the prototype 
supports product registration, QR -based verif ication, suspicious scan detection, and product revocation 
within the tested scenarios. The proposed system provides a practical approach for strengthening product 
authentication in prototype-level implementation. 
Keywords: Product authentication, blockcha in-based principles, counterfeit detection, clone detection, 
product revocation. 
 
1 Introduction 
The global market is increasingly affected by counterfeit goods, ranging from electronics to pharmaceuticals. 
These products not only result in significant economic losses for legitimate brands but also pose serious health 
and safety risks to consumers. Traditional anti-counterfeiting measures, such as holograms and simple barcodes, 
are often easily replicated by counterfeiters. Furthermore, centralized authentication systems present a single point 
of failure and may be vulnerable to data manipulation. 
Blockchain technology offers tamper-evident and transparent record-keeping principles that can support secure 
product authentication and supply chain traceabili ty. By recording important product events in a hash -linked 
structure, manufacturers can improve the integrity and auditability of product records. However, a critical 
limitation remains in the form of physical identifier cloning, particularly QR codes. A g enuine QR code can be 
duplicated and attached to counterfeit items, allowing them to pass basic verification processes if no additional 
detection mechanism is applied. 
To address this limitation, this paper proposes an enhanced product authentication system that applies blockchain-
based principles through a tamper -evident ledger, together with clone -risk detection and product revocation 
features. The clone-risk detection mechanism analyses scan location and timing patterns to identify suspicious 
verification behaviour, while the revocation feature allows compromised product identifiers to be invalidated. 
These enhancements provide a more reliable approach for prototype-level product authentication and counterfeit 
risk management.

2 Literature Review 
2.1 Product Authentication Systems 
Product authentication systems are widely used in industries that are highly vulnerable to counterfeiting, including 
pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, electronics, and luxury goods. These systems aim to enable stakeholders and 
consumers to verify the authenticity of products throughout their lifecycle. Among the various authentication 
methods, QR code–based verification has become one of the most commonly adopted approaches due to its low 
implementation cost, ease of deployment, and compatibility with mobile devices. 
QR codes allow manufacturers to assign a digital identifier to a product that can be scanned by consumers to 
retrieve product information and verification status. Despite these advantages, traditional QR codes are generally 
static and can be duplicated or reproduced without authorization. As a result, counterfeit products may carry 
copied QR codes that appear legitimate during verification. 
In addition to QR code authentication, many existing systems rely on centralized databases to store and manage 
authentication records. While centralized systems offer ease of administration and maintenance, they present 
limitations in term s of security, transparency, and trust. If the central database is compromised, authentication 
records may be altered, deleted, or manipulated, potentially undermining the reliability of the verification process. 
These challenges highlight the need for more secure and transparent approaches to product authentication. 
2.2 Blockchain Technology in Product Authentication 
Blockchain technology has emerged as a promising solution for improving product authentication due to its 
decentralization, transparency, traceability, and immutability. Blockchain systems store records in a 
cryptographically linked structure where each record is connected to previous records through hashing 
mechanisms. This structure makes unauthorized modifications significantly more difficult and enhances trust in 
stored information. 
The application of blockchain technology in product authentication has been widely explored in recent years. By 
recording product-related events on a blockchain, stakeholders can maintain transparent and tampe r-resistant 
records of product registration, ownership, verification, and distribution activities. These characteristics help 
improve auditability and provide stronger guarantees regarding data integrity. 
However, despite these benefits, several limitations remain in existing blockchain-based authentication systems. 
Many studies focus primarily on supply chain traceability and record management rather than direct consumer 
verification. Furthermore, although blockchain can protect the integrity of stored rec ords, it does not inherently 
prevent the duplication of physical product identifiers such as QR codes. 
As noted by Kulkarni et al. (2025), smart contracts can automate authentication and verification processes, 
improving efficiency and reducing reliance on manual operations. Nevertheless, if a QR code attached to a product 
is copied and reused on counterfeit products, multiple items may still appear authentic unless additional 
mechanisms are implemented to detect suspicious verification behaviour. Therefore, supplementary features such 
as clone detection and product revocation are necessary to strengthen authentication reliability.

2.4 Research Gap 
Based on the reviewed literature, several research gaps can be identified. 
Firstly, existing authentication systems provide limited support for effective clone detection. Although blockchain 
technology can secure authentication records, it does not prevent QR codes from being copied and reused on 
counterfeit products. Most existing systems focus on va lidating QR codes rather than detecting suspicious scan 
patterns that may indicate cloning. 
Secondly, product revocation capabilities remain limited. Once a product is identified as counterfeit, 
compromised, or recalled, many systems lack a structured mechanism to invalidate the product and communicate 
its status to consumers during future verification attempts. 
Thirdly, many studies focus primarily on supply chain traceability rather than end -consumer verification. 
Consequently, consumers often have limite d visibility into the authenticity status of products at the point of 
purchase or use. 
Finally, existing solutions rarely integrate authentication, clone detection, product revocation, and tamper-evident 
record keeping within a single platform. This limits their effectiveness in addressing modern counterfeit threats. 
Therefore, this study addresses these gaps by developing an enhanced product authentication system that 
integrates QR code verification, a blockchain -inspired tamper -evident ledger, clone detec tion, and product 
revocation features within a unified platform. 
3 Methodology and System Design 
3.1 Methodology  
This project adopts the System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) using the Waterfall Model, which provides a 
structured and linear process where each phase must be completed before moving to the next. The Waterfall 
approach is particularly suitable for the proposed product authentication system because the requirements are well 
defined and the security-related features require systematic development and testing 
3.2 System Overview 
The proposed system is a web-based product authentication platform designed to enhance counterfeit prevention 
through QR code verification, clone detection, and product revocation features. The system involves two primary 
user roles: manufacturers and consumers. 
Manufacturers register products, generate QR codes, and manage product status through an administrative 
interface. Consumers verify product authenticity by scanning QR codes using a mobile -friendly interface. The 
backend system processes verification requests, validates tokens, and analyzes scan behaviour. 
A tamper-evident ledger records key events such as product registration, verification, and revocation, ensuring 
data integrity and traceability. The system operates alongside a relational database, where detailed product 
information is stored.

3.3 System Architecture and Design 
The system architecture consists of three main components: the user interface, backend server, and data storage 
layer. The user interface includes both the admin dashboard and consumer verification page. The backend server 
handles authentication, QR code proces sing, and logic execution.  The data storage layer includes a relational 
database and a tamper-evident ledger. The database stores detailed product and user information, while the ledger 
records critical events in a chained structure using  hash-based linking to provide tamper -evident records and 
improve data integrity. 
 
Figure 3.1 System Architecture of the Proposed System 
3.4 System Workflow 
The system workflow begins with product registration by the manufacturer , followed by QR code generation. 
When a consumer scans the QR code, the system validates the token and retrieves product information. 
The system then checks the product status and analyses scan behaviour to detect potential cloning. Based on the 
verification result, the system displays the product status as authentic, suspicious, revoked, or invalid. 
 
Figure 3.2 Product Authentication Workflow 
3.5 Clone Detection Logic 
The clone-detection mechanism serves as a risk assessment tool and does not conclusively prove that a product is 
counterfeit. This includes monitoring repeated scans within short time intervals and scans from geographically 
distant locations.  
If abnormal patterns are detected, the system flags the product as suspicious. This approach enhances security by 
identifying duplicated QR codes that would otherwise pass standard verification.

3.6 Product Revocation Feature 
The product revocation feature allows manufacturers to invalidate compromised, recalled, or counterfeit products. 
Once revoked, the product status is updated in the system and recorded in the tamper-evident ledger. 
During verification, revoked products are clearly indicated to consumers, ensuring accurate and reliable 
authentication results. All revocation actions are recorded in the tamper -evident ledger to maintain traceability 
and auditability 
4 Implementation 
4.1 System Development 
The proposed system was developed as a web -based prototype that integrates QR code generation, signed QR 
payload validation, backend verification, scan logging, clone -risk detection, product revocation, and a tamper -
evident ledger. The system was designed to demonstrate a secure product authentication workflow while 
remaining suitable for prototype-level implementation. 
4.2 System Modules 
The implementation consists of two main modules: 
 Manufacturer Module: Allows manufacturers to log in, register products, generate secure QR codes, 
view product records, monitor scan activity, and manage product status, including product revocation. 
 Consumer Module: Provides a mobile-friendly verification interface that allows consumers to scan QR 
codes and verify product authenticity in real time.  
4.3 Backend Processing 
The backend system handles the main verification and management processe s, including manufacturer 
authentication, product registration, signed QR payload validation, product status checking, scan logging, clone-
risk analysis, revocation handling, analytics retrieval, and ledger recording. During verification, the backend 
validates the QR payload before retrieving product information. It then records scan metadata, analyses scan 
location and timing patterns, checks whether the product is active or revoked, and returns the appropriate 
verification result. 
4.4 Data Management 
The system uses a PostgreSQL relational database to store product records, QR identifiers, scan logs, revocation 
details, and ledger blocks. Product details and scan history are stored in relational tables, while important events 
such as product registration, veri fication, and revocation are recorded in a tamper -evident ledger. Each ledger 
block stores the event type, reference ID, timestamp, previous hash, and current hash, allowing the system to 
detect possible modification of recorded events. 
5 Testing and Evaluation 
The system was evaluated based on its functional performance and its ability to meet the defined objectives, 
including product registration, QR code verification, clone-risk detection, and product revocation handling. The 
testing focused on confirming whether the main system functions operated correctly within the prototype 
environment. 
5.1 Functional Testing 
Functional testing was conducted to ensure that the main system components operated correctly. This included 
testing manufacturer login, product regis tration, QR code generation, product listing, product verification, 
analytics viewing, and product revocation. The system successfully registered products, generated signed QR 
codes, and displayed the correct verification results for tested product cases.

5.2 Verification and Security Testing 
The QR code verification mechanism was tested using several scenarios, including valid, invalid, suspicious, and 
revoked product cases. The system correctly distinguished product status in the tested scenarios and displaye d 
appropriate verification results to the consumer. 
The clone -risk detection mechanism was evaluated by simulating scan behaviour involving different scan 
locations and timing patterns. The system analysed recent scan records and flagged suspicious behavio ur when 
the same QR code was scanned from geographically distant locations within an unrealistic time interval. Repeated 
scans from the same or nearby location were not automatically treated as suspicious, as they may represent normal 
consumer behaviour. 
The product revocation feature was tested by revoking selected products through the manufacturer interface. After 
revocation, the related QR code was deactivated, and subsequent verification attempts displayed the product as 
revoked. This confirmed that the revocation process was correctly reflected in the consumer verification result. 
5.3 Usability Evaluation 
Usability evaluation was conducted by observing interaction with the manufacturer and consumer interfaces. The 
manufacturer interface was evaluated based on product registration, QR code generation, product listing, analytics 
viewing, and revocation handling. The consumer interface was evaluated based on the clarity of verification results 
and ease of use on mobile devices. 
Overall, the system interface was  found to be understandable and suitable for prototype demonstration. The 
verification results were displayed clearly, allowing consumers to identify whether a product was authentic, 
suspicious, invalid, or revoked. 
5.4 Results and Discussion 
The evaluation re sults show that the proposed prototype was able to support the main product authentication 
workflow. The system successfully performed product registration, signed QR code generation, product 
verification, scan logging, clone-risk detection, product revocation, and tamper-evident ledger recording within 
the tested scenarios. 
The integration of clone-risk detection helps the system identify suspicious verification behaviour based on scan 
location and timing patterns, while the revocation feature ensures that  compromised or invalidated products are 
no longer displayed as authentic. Overall, the system demonstrates the feasibility of combining QR -based 
verification, behavioural risk detection, product revocation, and tamper-evident record keeping to support product 
authentication and counterfeit risk management. 
6 Conclusions 
This study proposes an enhanced product authentication system that applies blockchain -based principles by 
integrating secure QR code verification, clone -risk detection, product revocation, and tamper -evident ledger 
recording. By combining hash -linked record keeping with scan pattern analysis and manufacturer control, the 
system addresses key limitations of traditional authentication methods such as static barcodes, holograms, and 
centralized verification records. 
The results show that the prototype supports product registration, signed QR code generation, real -time 
verification, suspicious scan detection, and revocation of compromised products within the tested scenarios. These 
features demonstrate the feasibility of using QR-based verification, behavioural risk analysis, and tamper-evident 
record keeping to support product authentication and counterfeit risk management. 
Future work may include deployment on a decentralized blockchain network, integration with a dedicated mobile 
application, stronger authentication mechanisms, and improved clone -detection techniques to further enhance 
scalability, security, and usability.

Abstract - Counterfeit products continue to pose a significant challenge in modern supply chains, leading to 
financial losses and potential safety risks. Conventional authentication methods, such as QR codes and 
centralized databases, are vulnerable to duplication, data manipulation, and the absence of effective 
revocation mechan isms, which reduces consumer trust. This paper proposes an enhanced product 
authentication system that applies blockchain -based principles with integrated clone detection and product 
revocation features. Each product is assigned a unique code linked to a tamper-evident record to ensure data 
integrity and transparency. Scan activities are monitored to identify abnormal patterns that may indicate 
duplicated codes. A revocation mechanism enables invalidation of compromised or counterfeit products, 
ensuring accurate verification results. The system is implemented as a web -based prototype and evaluated 
based on functionality, verification accuracy, and system response. The results demonstrate improved security, 
enhanced counterfeit detection, and increased reliability compared to conventional approaches. The proposed 
system provides a practical and efficient solution for strengthening product authentication in real -world 
applications. 
Keywords: Product authentication, blockchain -based principles, counterfeit detec tion, clone detection, 
product revocation. 
 
1 Introduction 
The global market is increasingly saturated with counterfeit goods, ranging from electronics to pharmaceuticals. 
These products not only result in significant economic losses for legitimate brands but also pose serious health 
and safety risks to consumers. Traditional anti-counterfeiting measures, such as holograms and simple barcodes, 
are often easily replicated by sophisticated counterfeiters. Furthermore, centralized authentication systems present 
a single point of failure and are vulnerable to data manipulation. 
Blockchain technology offers a decentralized, tamper-evident, and transparent approach to securing supply chains. 
By recording product origins and ownership information on an immutable le dger, manufacturers can provide a 
digital traceability record for each product. However, a critical limitation remains in the form of physical identifier 
cloning, particularly QR codes. A genuine QR code can be duplicated and attached to counterfeit items, allowing 
them to pass basic verification processes. 
To address this limitation, this paper proposes an enhanced product authentication system that incorporates a clone 
detection mechanism based on scan frequency and geographical patterns, along with a product revocation feature. 
These enhancements enable the system to identify suspicious behaviour and invalidate compromised product 
identifiers, providing a more robust defence against modern counterfeiting techniques.

2 Literature Review 
2.1 Product Authentication Systems  
Product authentication systems are widely adopted in industries vulnerable to counterfeiting. QR code -based 
verification is commonly used due to its low cost and ease of implementation; however, QR codes are static and 
can be easily duplicated. Centralized database systems provide ease of management but suffer from limitations in 
security and transparency. Once a centralized system is compromised, authentication records can be manipulated, 
reducing system reliability. 
2.2 Blockchain Technology in Product Authentication  
Blockchain technology offers decentralization, immutability, and transparency, making it suitable for secure 
product authentication. Data recorded on a blockchain cannot be altered without consensus, thereby preventing 
unauthorized modifications. Despite these advantages, many blockchain-based systems lack real-time verification 
and effective revocation mechanisms for consumer-facing applications. 
As noted by Kulkar ni et al. (2025), smart contracts can automate verification processes; however, challenges 
remain in maintaining the integrity of the physical -to-digital link. If a physical identifier such as a QR code is 
cloned, the system may still validate multiple ins tances as authentic unless additional logic is implemented to 
detect anomalies. 
2.3 Research Gap 
Based on the literature, existing systems lack effective clone detection, dynamic revocation mechanisms, and 
consumer-friendly verification interfaces. Most studies focus on supply chain tracking rather than end-consumer 
verification and post-sale product integrity. 
These limitations highlight the need for an integrated solution that combines tamper-evident ledger mechanisms 
with real-time QR code verification, clon e detection, and product revocation features. Therefore, this study 
addresses these gaps by incorporating clone detection and revocation mechanisms into a blockchain-based product 
authentication system. 
3 Methodology and System Design 
3.1 Methodology  
This project adopts the System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) using the Waterfall Model, which provides a 
structured and linear process where each phase must be completed before moving to the next. The Waterfall 
approach is particularly suitable for a blockchain-based authentication system because the requirements are well 
defined and security is critical.  
3.2 System Overview 
The proposed system is a web-based product authentication platform designed to enhance counterfeit prevention 
through QR code verification, clone detection, and product revocation features. The system involves two primary 
user roles: manufacturers and consumers.

Manufacturers register products, generate QR codes, and manage product status through an administrative 
interface. Consumers verify product authenticity by scanning QR codes using a mobile -friendly interface. The 
backend system processes verification requests, validates tokens, and analyzes scan behaviour. 
A tamper-evident ledger records key events such as product registration, verification, a nd revocation, ensuring 
data integrity and traceability. The system operates alongside a relational database, where detailed product 
information is stored. 
3.3 System Architecture and Design  
The system architecture consists of three main components: the user interface, backend server, and data storage 
layer. The user interface includes both the admin dashboard and consumer verification page. The backend server 
handles authentication, QR code processing, and logic execution.  The data storage layer includes a re lational 
database and a tamper-evident ledger. The database stores detailed product and user information, while the ledger 
records critical events in a chained structure using hash-based linking to ensure integrity.

3.4 System Workflow 
The system workflow begins with product registration by the manufacturer, followed by QR code generation. 
When a consumer scans the QR code, the system validates the token and retrieves product information. 
The system then checks the product status and analyses scan behaviour to detect potential cloning. Based on the 
verification result, the system displays the product status as authentic, suspicious, revoked, or invalid.

3.5 Clone Detection Logic 
The clone detection mechanism analyzes scan behaviour to identify suspicious patterns. This includes monitoring 
repeated scans within short time intervals and scans from geographically distant locations. 
If abnormal patterns are detected, the system flags the product as suspicious. This approach enhances security by 
identifying duplicated QR codes that would otherwise pass standard verification. 
3.6 Product Revocation Feature  
The product revocation feature allows manufacturers to invalidate compromised, recalled, or counterfeit products. 
Once revoked, the product status is updated in the system and recorded in the tamper-evident ledger. 
During verification, revoked products are clearly indicated to consumers, ensuring accurate and reliable 
authentication results. 
4 Implementation 
4.1 System Development 
The proposed system was developed as a web -based prototype that integ rates QR code generation, backend 
verification, clone detection logic, and a tamper -evident ledger. The system is designed to simulate a secure 
product authentication environment while maintaining practical implementation constraints 
4.2 System Modules 
The implementation consists of two main modules: 
 Admin Module: Allow manufacturers to register products, generate QR codes, and manage product 
status, including revocation. 
 Consumer Module: Provides a mobile-friendly interface for users to scan QR codes and verify product 
authenticity in real time. 
4.3 Backend Processing 
The backend system handles all verification processes, including QR token validation, product status checking, 
and scan behaviour analysis. It also manages interactions between the database and the tamper-evident ledger. 
4.4 Data Management 
The system uses a relational database to store product and user information, while the tamper -evident ledger 
records critical events such as product registration, verification, and revocation to ensure data integrity an d 
traceability. 
5 Testing and Evaluation 
The system was evaluated based on its functional performance and its ability to meet the defined objectives, 
including product registration, verification accuracy, clone detection, and revocation handling. 
5.1 Functional Testing 
Functional testing was conducted to ensure that all system components operated correctly. This includes validating 
product registration, QR code generation, and the verification process. The system successfully identified valid 
and invalid QR codes, demonstrating consistent and reliable authentication performance. 
5.2 Verification and Security Testing  
The QR code verification mechanism was tested under multiple scenarios, including valid, invalid, and revoked 
product cases. The system achieved high accuracy in distinguishing product status, ensuring correct classification 
during verification.

The clone detection mechanism was evaluated by simulating repeated scans and abnormal access patterns. A 
predefined threshold based on scan frequency and behavi oural variation was applied to identify suspicious 
activity. The results indicate that the system effectively detects duplicated QR code usage and flags it as 
suspicious. 
The product revocation feature was also tested by marking selected products as revoked. During verification, all 
revoked products were correctly identified and displayed, confirming the reliability of the revocation mechanism. 
5.3 Usability Evaluation 
Usability testing was conducted with multiple users to evaluate system interaction. Most users were able to 
complete verification tasks successfully with minimal guidance. Feedback indicates that the system interface is 
user-friendly, with clear and understandable authentication results. 
Overall, users reported a high level of satisfaction and confidence when using the system for product verification. 
5.4 Results and Discussion 
The evaluation results demonstrate that the proposed system achieves reliable product authentication and 
effectively addresses key limitations of traditional methods. The in tegration of clone detection enhances the 
system’s ability to identify duplicated QR codes, while the revocation feature ensures that compromised products 
are accurately flagged.  Overall, the system improves transparency, strengthens counterfeit prevention , and 
increases user confidence in product authenticity. 
6 Conclusions 
This study proposes an enhanced product authentication system that applies blockchain -based principles by 
integrating QR code verification with clone detection and product revocation features. By combining immutability 
with scan pattern analysis and administrative control, the system addresses key limitations of traditional 
authentication methods such as barcodes, holograms, and centralized databases. 
The results indicate that the system i s capable of providing secure product registration, real -time verification, 
detection of suspicious duplicate scans, and effective revocation of compromised products. These features 
collectively improve transparency, strengthen counterfeit prevention, and enhance consumer trust in modern 
supply chains. 
Future work may include deployment on a live blockchain network and integration with mobile applications to 
further improve scalability and usability.
